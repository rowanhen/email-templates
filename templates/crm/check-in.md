# Check-In

A periodic touchpoint with existing customers. Not sales, not support — purely relationship. The goal is to stay present without being annoying.

## When to Send

- 30, 60, or 90 days after onboarding completes
- Quarterly for long-term accounts
- After a period of low/no product activity (use as a soft re-engagement signal)

## Key Elements

- **No CTA to buy** — this is the one email where you explicitly don't pitch
- **Personalised usage hook** — reference something specific to their account if possible
- **Open question** — invite a reply; genuine curiosity, not a form
- **Short** — 3–5 sentences max; any longer signals it's not really a check-in

## Non-Obvious Patterns

- **Send from a human, not a role address** — "from Sarah at Acme" outperforms "from Acme Support"
- **Reference their industry or use case**, not your product features — "how's Q1 going for your team?" beats "are you using our new dashboard?"
- **Low-activity customers need a different variant** — acknowledge the gap, don't pretend they've been active: "We noticed things have been quiet — is there anything blocking you?"
- **The best check-ins feel like texts, not emails** — informal, brief, human

## Common Mistakes

- Disguising a sales email as a check-in (customers see through it immediately)
- Sending at scale with obvious mail-merge tells (wrong name, wrong product feature)
- Making it easy to unsubscribe but hard to reply — you want the reply
- Asking three questions instead of one

## Typical Structure

```
Subject: Checking in — how's it going?

Hi {{name}},

Just wanted to check in — how are things going with {{product_or_use_case}}?

[Optional: We noticed {{light personalisation — e.g. "you've been running X reports"}} — hope it's been useful.]

Is there anything you've been stuck on, or anything we could do better?

Happy to hop on a call if that's easier.

— {{csm_or_founder_name}}
```
