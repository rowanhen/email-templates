# Win-Back Email

Re-engaging a user who has already churned — cancelled, lapsed, or gone dark for 30–90+ days. Harder than churn prevention; works best with a genuine reason to come back.

## When to Send

- 30 days post-cancellation (first attempt); 60–90 days (second if no response)
- After a significant product update that addresses why they likely left
- Avoid: immediately after cancellation (too raw) or >6 months (likely irrelevant)
- ⚠️ **GDPR/CAN-SPAM:** Do not email users who opted out of marketing at cancellation — this is a legal requirement, not a best practice. Check consent records before sending.

## Best Practices

- Acknowledge the gap — don't pretend they never left
- Segment by cancellation reason (price, missing feature, competitor) and tailor the message
- "New feature" win-backs significantly outperform generic "we miss you" emails
- Reference the exit reason if you collected it — it shows you were listening
- Limit to 2 attempts — then remove from win-back sequences

## Common Mistakes

- Immediate post-cancel win-back (within 24–48h) — looks desperate
- Vague "we've made improvements" without specifics — give a real reason why now is different
- Long email that reads like a marketing newsletter

## Typical Structure

```
Subject: A lot has changed since you left, [Name]

Hi [Name],

It's been [X months] since you cancelled [Product]. Wanted to
share what's new — in case any of it changes things.

Since [month/year] we've shipped:
- [Feature 1 — tie to their known pain if possible]
- [Feature 2]
- [Pricing/plan change if relevant]

If [original reason for leaving] was the issue, worth another look.

[CTA: "Reactivate with 30 days free" or "See what's new →"]

No pressure — just wanted you to know. [Name]
```

## Metrics to Track

- **Win-back rate** — churned users who reactivate within 30 days
- **Reactivation retention** — do win-backs stick, or churn again within 90 days?
- **Revenue recovered** — ARR/MRR from win-back campaigns
