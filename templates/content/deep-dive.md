# Deep Dive

Single-topic editorial or feature email. Long-form, one subject explored thoroughly — the opposite of a roundup. Builds authority and rewards loyal readers.

## When to Send

- When a topic warrants more than a paragraph
- As a standalone issue in a newsletter or content series
- When you have a genuine insight, not just a summary

## Key Elements

- **A single, clearly scoped subject** — narrow enough to cover well, not a broad survey
- **Strong opening hook** — a counter-intuitive claim, question, or data point
- **Structured argument or narrative** — with headers or clear progression
- **Concrete examples** — abstract claims need grounding
- **A clear takeaway or conclusion** — what should the reader think or do differently?

## Best Practices

- **Commit to depth** — if you can't go deeper than a blog post summary, don't call it a deep dive
- **Use headers** — long text without structure loses readers; break it into scannable sections
- **One conclusion** — don't hedge; deep dives earn trust by taking a position
- **Link to sources** — credibility comes from showing your work

## Common Mistakes

- Trying to cover too many angles in one email
- Long without being deep — lots of words, no new insight
- No clear structure, reads as a wall of text
- Burying the core argument in the middle

## Typical Structure

```
Subject: [Specific claim or provocative question about the topic]

[Opening hook — surprising fact, counter-intuitive framing, or a question]

## [Section 1 heading]
[Body — 2–4 paragraphs]

## [Section 2 heading]
[Body — 2–4 paragraphs]

## [Conclusion heading]
[What to take away. Your opinion.]

— [Author name]
```

## Metrics to Track

- Read time / scroll depth (if measurable)
- Reply rate (high-quality content drives replies)
- Forwards and shares
