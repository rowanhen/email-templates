# Breakup Email

The final email in an outbound sequence — you're closing the loop. Counterintuitively, these often get the highest reply rates in a sequence.

## When to Send

- Final email in a cold sequence (typically email 4 or 5)
- After a proposal has gone dark for 2+ weeks

## Best Practices

- Keep it 3–5 lines max — brevity is the whole mechanism
- Be genuine about closing the loop (don't fake it)
- Leave the door open ("if timing changes") without expectation
- Send mid-week, mid-morning — same logic as cold outreach timing

## Common Mistakes

- Writing a breakup email that's actually just another pitch
- Guilt-tripping ("I've tried reaching you 5 times...")
- Implying resentment or making them feel guilty — keep the tone warm and genuine
- Going too long — destroys the effect
- Using the breakup email too early in the sequence

## Typical Structure

**Cold sequence close-out:**
```
Subject: Re: [original thread] — closing the loop

Hi [Name],

I've reached out a few times and haven't heard back — going to
stop here rather than keep nudging you.

If things change, you know where to find me.

[Name]
```

**After a gone-dark proposal:**
```
Subject: Re: [Proposal] — should I close this out?

Hi [Name],

It's been [X weeks] since I sent the proposal and I haven't heard back.
Should I close this out? If timing's shifted, just say the word.

[Name]
```

## Metrics to Track

- **Reply rate** — breakup emails often hit 10–20% reply rate
- **Recovered deals** — how many "wait, actually..." replies convert
