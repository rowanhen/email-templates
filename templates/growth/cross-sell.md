# Cross-Sell Email

Recommends adjacent products, add-ons, or features to existing customers based on what they already use. Works because the trust is already there.

## When to Send

- After 30–60 days of active usage of the core product
- When a user's behaviour patterns match the profile of customers who use Product B
- After a key milestone (first export, first integration, first report)
- **Avoid:** within onboarding window or during any billing issue

## Key Elements

- **Usage hook** — acknowledge what they already do ("You've been using [Feature A]...")
- **The connection** — why the adjacent product is a natural next step
- **Social proof** — "X% of [Feature A] users also use [Feature B]"
- **Low-friction entry** — free trial, one-click enable, or no extra cost

## Best Practices

- Ground the recommendation in *their* actual usage, not generic upsell copy
- One recommendation per email — not a product catalogue
- "Customers like you" framing outperforms generic feature lists
- Works best when the cross-sell is free or low-cost to start

## Common Mistakes

- Recommending products irrelevant to their use case
- Treating cross-sell as an upsell — it's about breadth, not tier
- Pushing paid add-ons before the customer has seen full value from core product
- Multiple product pitches in one email

## Typical Structure

```
Subject: [Name], you might love [Product B]

Hi [Name],

You've been getting a lot out of [Feature A] — nice work.

A lot of [Feature A] users also turn on [Feature B] around this point.
It [does X], which means [benefit tied to what they're already doing].

[Customer name] started using it and [specific result].

[CTA: "Enable [Feature B] free →" or "See how it works →"]

Takes 2 minutes to set up.
```

## Metrics to Track

- **Adoption rate** — % of recipients who enable/purchase the cross-sell
- **Time to adopt** — days from email to first use
- **Retention impact** — do cross-sell adopters churn less?
- **Revenue per customer** — ACV lift from cross-sell campaigns
