# Employee Onboarding Email (Day 1)

Welcome email sent to a new hire on their first day. Sets the tone, reduces anxiety, and gets them oriented without overwhelming them.

## When to Send
- Day 1 morning, at or before the start of their working hours
- Consider a pre-boarding email 1–3 days before start for logistics (laptop, first-day location, who to ask for)
- Remote hires: this email carries more weight — it may be their primary first impression

## Who Sends It
- **From the direct manager:** personal welcome + team context
- **From People/HR:** company-wide logistics (systems, policies, first-week schedule)
- Both is fine — coordinate to avoid duplication

## Best Practices
- Name the 3–5 things they need to do on day 1, in order — decision fatigue is highest on day 1
- Include the name and Slack/Teams handle of their onboarding buddy — "ask your manager" is a deterrent; a named buddy is a resource
- Don't attach the full employee handbook — link to it
- For remote hires: explicitly state what "a good first day looks like" — no office cues to follow
- Keep tone warm but not over-the-top — excessive enthusiasm reads as performative

## Typical Structure
```
Subject: Welcome to [Company], [Name] — your day 1 guide

Hi [Name],

Welcome! Here's what your first day looks like:

Day 1 checklist:
1. Set up your laptop — [IT setup guide →]
2. Join Slack and say hi in #introductions
3. 10am: intro call with [Manager] — [calendar link]
4. Read the [First Week Guide →] (20 min)
5. 2pm: team standup — [link]

Your go-to for anything this week: [Buddy name] (@handle on Slack)

Important links:
• [HR portal / benefits — enrol by: X]
• [IT helpdesk: it@company.com / #it-help]
• [Team handbook →]

[Manager name] · [Title] · [Phone/Slack]
```

## Metrics to Track
- **Day-1 IT/HR support ticket volume** — high volume = onboarding email missing critical info
- **New hire survey score (30 days)** — "Did you feel well-prepared on your first day?" directly reflects this email's quality
- **Time-to-productivity at 30/60/90 days** — correlates with clarity of early onboarding communications
