# Internal Newsletter

Regular digest for employees — company updates, team wins, useful reads, and cross-team visibility. Builds culture and reduces information silos.

## When to Send
- Weekly or bi-weekly is standard; monthly for smaller or lower-velocity teams
- Fixed day and time — predictability increases opens (Friday afternoon or Monday morning work well)
- If you miss a send: skip it, don't double up the following week

## Who Curates It
- Assign a named curator with authority to cut content — without editorial ownership, length balloons and the newsletter dies
- Contributors submit by a set deadline (e.g., Wednesday EOD for Friday send)

## Best Practices
- Cap at 5–7 items — a newsletter nobody finishes is worse than a shorter one everyone reads
- Use consistent section headings every issue so readers can scan predictably ("Company", "Team Wins", "Reminders", "Worth Reading")
- "Team Wins" is consistently the highest-engagement section — recognition drives reads
- Include one non-work item (interesting read, fun fact) — it's the thing people mention out loud
- Archive every issue — new hires need this history to onboard to company culture

## Common Mistakes
- No editorial ownership — anyone dumps content in, length explodes, nobody reads it
- One-way broadcast with no reply mechanism — ask a question or run a poll occasionally

## Typical Structure
```
Subject: [Company] Weekly — [W/C date]

📢 Company
• [Update — 1 sentence + link if needed]

🏆 Team Wins
• [Person/team] shipped [thing] — [outcome]

📅 Coming Up
• [Event / deadline] — [date]

🔗 Worth Reading
• [Article / doc] — [why it's relevant in one line]

💬 From the Team
[Question, poll, or event photo]

---
Submit for next week by Wednesday EOD → #newsletter-submissions
[Archive →]
```

## Metrics to Track
- **Open rate** — aim for 75%+ for internal newsletters (external benchmarks of 60%+ don't apply; internal audiences have higher baseline expectation); steady decline = content quality or frequency problem
- **Click rate** — shows whether content generates genuine interest vs. passive opens
- **Submission volume** — how many people contribute; low = low ownership of company culture
