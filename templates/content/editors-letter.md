# Editor's Letter

Personal, opinion-driven message from a real person. A relationship-building pattern that puts a human voice at the front of a brand or publication.

## When to Send

- As the opening of a newsletter issue
- During significant moments (milestones, shifts in direction, big news)
- When you want to re-establish connection with an audience

## Key Elements

- **A real name and voice** — this must feel personal, not corporate
- **First-person perspective** — opinions, observations, reflections
- **A clear point of view** — not a summary; an actual stance
- **Brief closing** — sign off like a person, not a brand

## Best Practices

- **Write it, don't produce it** — editor's letters that feel committee-approved defeat the purpose
- **Short is fine** — 100–250 words is often better than 500
- **Reveal something** — a behind-the-scenes view, a personal admission, a genuine reaction
- **Don't pitch** — save the CTA for elsewhere; this section earns trust, not clicks

## Common Mistakes

- Ghostwritten in obvious brand voice — defeats authenticity
- Using it as an intro to a promotional email
- Being vague ("exciting things ahead!") without substance
- Signing off with a logo instead of a name

## Typical Structure

```
Subject: {{Personal subject line — a thought, not a product name}}

Hi {{first_name or "there"}},

{{Personal opening — what's on your mind this week/month}}

{{Your take — 2–3 sentences of genuine opinion or observation}}

{{Optional: what's in this issue, briefly}}

— {{Real name}}, {{Title}}
```

## Metrics to Track

- Reply rate (the best signal for a personal email)
- Open rate vs. other email types
- Unsubscribe rate (poor letters increase it)
