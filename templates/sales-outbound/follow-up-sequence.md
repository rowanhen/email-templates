# Follow-Up Sequence

A multi-touch cadence of 3–5 emails over 2 weeks after cold outreach with no response. Each touchpoint adds new value rather than just bumping the thread.

## When to Send

- After cold outreach gets no reply within 3–4 business days
- Each follow-up spaced 3–5 days apart
- Stop after 4–5 attempts unless you get a signal (open, click, reply)

## Cadence Structure

| Email | Timing | Angle |
|-------|--------|-------|
| #1 | Day 0 | Cold outreach (see `cold-outreach.md`) |
| #2 | Day 4 | Different angle / new hook |
| #3 | Day 8 | Short value-add (case study, insight) |
| #4 | Day 12 | Bump with humour or bluntness |
| #5 | Day 15 | Breakup email (see `breakup-email.md`) |

## Best Practices

- Reply to the original thread — keeps context and signals continuity (not a fresh cold blast)
- Each email should be readable standalone (recipient may not scroll up)
- Mix email with LinkedIn touches between sends

## Common Mistakes

- "Just checking in" with no new content — worthless
- Too many emails too fast — 2 days apart looks desperate
- Giving up after one follow-up — most replies come at email 3–4
- Apologising for following up ("Sorry to bother you again...")

## Typical Structure

**Email 2:**
```
Subject: Re: [original subject]

Hi [Name],

[New observation or angle]. [One sentence connecting to a pain you solve.]

Still relevant for [Company]? Happy to chat.
```
**Email 4:**
```
Subject: Re: [original subject]

[Name] — reached out a few times. Timing off or not relevant right now?

Either way, totally fair. Just let me know and I'll stop.
```

## Metrics to Track

- **Reply rate by email number** — find where your sequence drops off
- **Unfinished sequence rate** — high completion = few replies; use to spot dead sequences
- **Meeting booked rate** — which email in the sequence converts most
