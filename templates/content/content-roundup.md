# Content Roundup

Curated links and resources grouped by theme. The "This week in X" pattern — an aggregation email that saves readers time by filtering signal from noise.

## When to Send

- On a consistent schedule (weekly, biweekly, or monthly)
- When you have 4–8 genuinely useful items to share
- Tied to a theme or topic your audience actively follows

## Key Elements

- **Consistent subject line format** — readers should recognise it instantly ("This week in…", "The [Name] Digest")
- **Brief intro paragraph** — one or two sentences, your editorial take on the week
- **Grouped items with context** — not just links; a one-sentence explanation of why each item matters
- **Consistent item count** — don't balloon to 20 items; curate ruthlessly

## Best Practices

- **Edit, don't aggregate** — a roundup is only valuable if you've filtered; five great links beat fifteen mediocre ones
- **Add your voice** — readers subscribe for your perspective, not a list they could Google
- **Use consistent formatting per item** — headline, source, one-line summary
- **Keep the intro brief** — readers came for the links; don't bury them

## Common Mistakes

- Including filler links to hit a count target
- No editorial framing — just a list of URLs with no context
- Inconsistent sending schedule (destroys the habit loop)
- Subject lines that don't signal it's a roundup

## Typical Structure

```
Subject: This week in {{topic}} — {{date}}

{{One-paragraph editorial intro — your take on the week}}

🔗 {{Item title}}
{{Source}} — {{One sentence: why this matters}}

🔗 {{Item title}}
{{Source}} — {{One sentence: why this matters}}

[... repeat for each item]

— {{Author name}}
```

## Metrics to Track

- Open rate (benchmark consistency week-over-week)
- Click rate per item (reveals what resonates)
- Subscriber growth and list churn
